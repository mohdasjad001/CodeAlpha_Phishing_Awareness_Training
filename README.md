# CodeAlpha_Phishing_Awareness_Training
# Overview

This repository contains the **Phishing Awareness Training** module developed as part of the CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship. Phishing relies on social engineering rather than software exploits, tricking users into sharing credentials, financial details, or sensitive access through deceptive messages and look-alike websites.

The training deck walks learners through how phishing attacks unfold, how to spot red flags in emails and URLs, psychological triggers used by attackers, realistic scenarios, preventive security habits, and a quick knowledge check.



# Training Deck Breakdown (12 Slides)

* Slide 1 — Title & Overview: Phishing Awareness Training — Spot it. Stop it. Report it.
* Slide 2 — Introduction to Phishing: Core definition, targeted assets (credentials, banking/card details, personal data and system access), and attack channels (email, SMS, calls, social media, fake websites).
* Slide 3 — How Phishing Attacks Work: Five-stage attack chain (Attacker plans → Message sent → Victim clicks → Data stolen → Account misused) and why pausing before clicking breaks the chain.
* Slide 4 — Types of Phishing Attacks: Email phishing, spear phishing, whaling, smishing, vishing, and clone phishing.
* Slide 5 — How to Identify Phishing Emails: Spotting look-alike sender domains, urgency/threats, generic greetings, mismatched links, spelling errors, and unexpected attachments.
* Slide 6 — How to Identify Fake Websites: Comparing legitimate domains against deceptive URLs, checking domain spelling/subdomains/TLDs, understanding HTTPS limitations, typing official URLs directly, and avoiding suspicious pop-ups.
* Slide 7 — Social Engineering Techniques: Urgency, fear, authority, reward, curiosity, pretexting, and baiting.
* Slide 8 — Real-World Phishing Examples: Fictionalized scenarios with red flags—fake courier/delivery SMS, fake IT mailbox alert, and fake prize/card solicitation.
* Slide 9 — Best Practices to Prevent Phishing: Think before clicking, verify via official channels, enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), use strong unique passwords/password manager, keep software updated, and report suspicious messages.
* Slide 10 — Interactive Awareness Quiz: Three multiple-choice scenario questions testing detection and response habits.
* Slide 11 — Key Takeaways: Four-step response loop (STOP → THINK → VERIFY → REPORT) and core rule that phishing targets people, not computers.
* Slide 12 — Closing: Summary wrap-up (Stay alert. Stay secure.).

# How to Use This Module

1. Download or open `Phishing_Awareness_Training.pptx` (or `Phishing_Awareness_Training.pdf` for quick viewing).
2. Present slide by slide to students, employees, or general users.
3. During **Slide 10 (Interactive Awareness Quiz)**, pause for audience answers before revealing the options/answers.
4. Reinforce the **STOP → THINK → VERIFY → REPORT** workflow at the end of the session.
